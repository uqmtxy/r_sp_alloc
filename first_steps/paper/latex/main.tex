\begin{abstract}
Dedicated deep-learning inference pipelines such as ONNX Runtime executing YOLO26n cache large intermediate tensor arenas across inference bursts. While caching maximizes intra-burst throughput, unreturned free pages inflate process physical memory (\texttt{RSS} / \texttt{phys\_footprint}) by $2\times$--$3\times$ during idle gaps and across staggered multi-camera deployments. Conversely, naive per-frame OS unbacking (\texttt{MADV\_DONTNEED} or allocator trimming) introduces thousands of minor page faults per burst, and on macOS Apple Silicon (\texttt{16~KiB} pages), standard POSIX \texttt{MADV\_DONTNEED} and \texttt{MADV\_FREE} are kernel no-ops that leave physical footprint unchanged.

We present a cross-OS page-grid arena architecture combining (1) \texttt{PageReuseResource}, an exact-boundary allocator tracking \textit{head}, \textit{body}, \textit{tail}, and \textit{sub-page} residency across both \texttt{4~KiB} Linux and \texttt{16~KiB} macOS XNU virtual memory via \texttt{mmap(MAP\_FIXED)} anonymous remapping; (2) \texttt{IdleReclamationController}, a 2-competitive online idle-threshold watchdog with frame-boundary instrumentation; and (3) \texttt{GlobalBudgetArbiter}, a cooperative multi-stream arbiter that sheds least-recently-active idle streams synchronously at burst handoff, unified in a hybrid policy (\texttt{pool\_hybrid}). We prove five formal theorems governing residency conservation, 2-competitive online idle reclamation, multi-stream duty-cycle scaling, the linear fault--budget tradeoff law, and hybrid dominance across back-to-back vs.\ sparse bursts, verifying all five programmatically against raw experimental traces ($R^2 = 0.9999998$ on \texttt{4~KiB} Linux; $R^2 = 0.999875$ on \texttt{16~KiB} macOS). Across paired bare-metal CloudLab \texttt{m510} experiments (\texttt{4~KiB}), a \texttt{24~MiB} bounded pool cuts burst idle private RSS to $0.4916\times$ ($58.37$ vs.\ $118.79\text{ MiB}$ over 1,000 endurance frames) at $1.0108\times$ median burst $p_{99}$ latency. Across 181 validated Apple M2 Pro experiments (\texttt{16~KiB}, bit-exact FP32 outputs), single-stream \texttt{pool\_auto} cuts idle \texttt{phys\_footprint} from $105.8\text{ MiB}$ (\texttt{BFC}) to $42.3\text{ MiB}$ with zero false reclaims and achieves $1.59\times$ empirical competitive ratio at $\tau^*=250\text{ ms}$ (vs.\ $2.72\times$ retain); under staggered 8-camera handoff (\texttt{N=8, K=2}), \texttt{pool\_hybrid} caps peak \texttt{phys\_footprint} at $225.8\text{ MiB}$ vs.\ $670.7\text{ MiB}$ (\texttt{BFC}), $415.1\text{ MiB}$ (\texttt{pool\_retain}), and $289.0\text{ MiB}$ (\texttt{pool\_auto\_0}), while incurring $8.0\times$ fewer minor page faults than \texttt{direct\_jemalloc} ($32{,}412$ vs.\ $258{,}802$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Edge computer vision appliances---ranging from automated optical inspection stations to multi-camera surveillance hubs---frequently run neural inference in bursty or staggered duty cycles. Frameworks such as ONNX Runtime rely on Best-Fit with Coalescing (\texttt{BFC}) arenas that pre-allocate and retain tens of megabytes of virtual address space per session so tensor allocations avoid OS kernel round-trips. While effective for continuous single-stream throughput, indefinite retention imposes two severe costs:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Multi-Stream Physical Footprint Multiplication:} When $N$ camera streams share a host but only $K \ll N$ burst concurrently (e.g., motion-triggered or time-sliced scheduling at duty cycle $d = K/N$), static arenas hoard $N \cdot M_{\text{arena}}$ physical DRAM even though at most $K \cdot M_{\text{arena}}$ is actively needed.
  \item \textbf{Granularity Mismatch and OS Reclamation Divergence:} As highlighted by Fistanto et al.~\cite{fistanto2023lostbytes}, user-space allocators routinely misalign free spans with OS page frames, trapping unreturnable \textit{head}, \textit{tail}, and \textit{sub-page} bytes. Worse, page-reclamation syscalls are not portable: while Linux \texttt{madvise(MADV\_DONTNEED)} immediately unbacks \texttt{4~KiB} anonymous pages, Apple XNU (\texttt{16~KiB} pages on Apple Silicon) treats both \texttt{MADV\_DONTNEED} and \texttt{MADV\_FREE} as no-ops for private \texttt{phys\_footprint}.
\end{enumerate}

A second pitfall in allocator research is conflating \emph{allocator-local utilization} with \emph{process-level physical footprint}. Under synthetic exact-fit workloads, a page-grid slab can report $99.99\%$ allocator-local utilization while process-level \texttt{RSS} still includes runtime weights, framework thread stacks, and OS heap fragmentation---and in continuous single-stream execution, aggressive per-frame trimming (\texttt{direct\_trim}) can even slightly edge out \texttt{pool\_eager\_trim} on active peak RSS ($73.39$ vs.\ $73.74\text{ MiB}$ on CloudLab). Meaningful memory optimization for dedicated inference therefore requires \emph{workload-aware temporal and cooperative reclamation} grounded in process-level physical accounting (\texttt{PSS}/private RSS on Linux; Mach \texttt{phys\_footprint} on macOS).

\paragraph{Contributions.} This paper makes four contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Formal Analytical Framework (Section~\ref{sec:theory}):} We state and prove five theorems covering (1) exact page-residency conservation and $2P$-per-span boundary waste, (2) a 2-competitive online idle-threshold bound via ski-rental analysis, (3) multi-stream duty-cycle footprint scaling $R_{\text{base}}(N) + K \cdot M_{\text{arena}}$, (4) the linear minor-fault vs.\ retained-budget law $F(B) = F_0 - \text{slope}\cdot B$, and (5) hybrid dominance across back-to-back vs.\ sparse bursts.
  \item \textbf{Cross-OS Page-Grid Arena \& Hybrid Arbiter (Section~\ref{sec:design}):} We implement \texttt{PageReuseResource}, \texttt{IdleReclamation\allowbreak Controller}, and \texttt{GlobalBudgetArbiter} (\texttt{pool\_hybrid}) in C++17, abstracting Linux \texttt{MADV\_\allowbreak DONTNEED} (\texttt{4~KiB}) and XNU \texttt{mmap(MAP\_FIXED)} (\texttt{16~KiB}) behind a unified page-conservation interface.
  \item \textbf{Dual-Platform Empirical Validation (Section~\ref{sec:eval}):} We evaluate live ONNX Runtime YOLO26n~\cite{ultralytics2026yolo26} inference with bit-exact FP32 verification across bare-metal CloudLab \texttt{m510} (\texttt{4~KiB}) and Apple M2 Pro (\texttt{16~KiB}, 181 new protocol-validated runs), strictly separating hardware baselines.
  \item \textbf{Reproducible Verification Suite:} Every theorem and claim in this paper is backed by automated assertions in \texttt{scripts/research/\allowbreak verify\_theory.py} and \texttt{context/\allowbreak THEORY\_VERIFICATION.json}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and OS VM Pitfalls}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Granularity Mismatch vs.\ Process Footprint}
Following Fistanto et al.~\cite{fistanto2023lostbytes}, any free contiguous byte range $[a, b)$ aligned within virtual address space decomposes relative to OS page size $P$ into at most four disjoint classes: a partial leading \emph{head} ($< P$), zero or more full interior \emph{body} pages ($k \cdot P$), a partial trailing \emph{tail} ($< P$), and a \emph{sub-page} span when $a$ and $b$ lie within the same page. Only \emph{body} pages can be safely returned to the OS without corrupting live adjacent allocations.

\subsection{Why POSIX \texttt{madvise} Fails on Apple Silicon}
Before deploying our arena on macOS arm64 (\texttt{16~KiB} hardware pages), we conducted a controlled virtual-memory probe: we allocated a \texttt{32~MiB} anonymous \texttt{mmap} region, faulted all pages, freed the upper \texttt{16~MiB} half while keeping the lower \texttt{16~MiB} live neighbor intact, and measured both \texttt{mincore()} residency and Mach \texttt{task\_vm\_info.phys\_footprint}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{MADV\_DONTNEED} and \texttt{MADV\_FREE}: Both are XNU no-ops on private anonymous mappings (\texttt{phys\_footprint} remains $32 \to 32\text{ MiB}$, $\Delta = 0$).
  \item \texttt{MADV\_FREE\_REUSABLE}: Drops accounted \texttt{phys\_footprint} ($32 \to 16\text{ MiB}$), but leaves \texttt{mincore()} page-table bits set ($32\text{ MiB}$) for lazy kernel reclaim under global pressure.
  \item \texttt{mmap(..., MAP\_PRIVATE | MAP\_ANON | MAP\_FIXED, -1, 0)}: Atomically replaces the backing VM object on aligned interior pages, immediately dropping \emph{both} \texttt{mincore()} and \texttt{phys\_footprint} ($32 \to 16\text{ MiB}$) while preserving live neighbor bytes verbatim.
\end{itemize}
Figure~\ref{fig:fig1}(b) summarizes this divergence, which necessitates our OS-specific reclamation backend.

\section{Formal Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $P \in \{4096, 16384\}$ denote the OS hardware page size in bytes, and let an arena manage a contiguous virtual reservation partitioned into live allocations and coalesced free spans $\{S_1, \dots, S_m\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Page-Residency Conservation and Boundary Waste]
\label{thm:conservation}
For any sequence of allocations and coalesced deallocations with OS page size $P$, total resident bytes $R$ satisfy the exact conservation identity
\begin{equation}
  R = R_{\text{live}} + R_{\text{head}} + R_{\text{tail}} + R_{\text{body}} + R_{\text{sub}},
\end{equation}
where $R_{\text{live}}$ is the page-rounded residency of live objects. Invoking \texttt{purge\_to\_budget(B)} preserves $R_{\text{live}}$ identically, enforces $R_{\text{body}} \le B$, and leaves unpurgeable free-span boundary waste bounded by
\begin{equation}
  R_{\text{head}} + R_{\text{tail}} + R_{\text{sub}} < 2mP.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each virtual page $p$ overlapping the arena belongs to one of two disjoint sets: either $p$ intersects at least one live object (contributing $P$ bytes to $R_{\text{live}}$), or $p$ lies strictly inside a coalesced free span $S_i = [a_i, b_i)$. Within $S_i$, alignment to $P$-byte boundaries partitions resident pages into at most one partial leading page ($\text{head}_i < P$), $\lfloor (b'_i - a'_i)/P \rfloor$ full interior pages ($\text{body}_i$), and at most one partial trailing page ($\text{tail}_i < P$); if $S_i$ spans no page boundary, it contributes $\text{sub}_i < P$. Summing over $i=1,\dots,m$ yields $R = R_{\text{live}} + R_{\text{head}} + R_{\text{tail}} + R_{\text{body}} + R_{\text{sub}}$. Because \texttt{purge\_to\_budget(B)} only issues OS unbacking (\texttt{MADV\_DONTNEED} on Linux; \texttt{mmap(MAP\_FIXED)} on XNU) on full interior pages of free spans, no live object page is touched ($R_{\text{live}}$ is invariant), interior pages are unbacked FIFO until $R_{\text{body}} \le B$, and each span retains strictly less than $2P$ boundary bytes (since $\text{head}_i + \text{tail}_i < 2P$ when $\text{sub}_i=0$, and $\text{sub}_i < P < 2P$ otherwise).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[2-Competitive Online Idle Reclamation]
\label{thm:competitive}
Consider a single stream with reclaimable free body $M_{\text{free}}$, holding cost rate $c_{\text{hold}}$ per byte-second, and total purge-plus-refault overhead $C_{\text{refault}} = c_{\text{sys}} + c_{\text{fault}}(M_{\text{free}}/P)$. Setting the online idle timer to the break-even threshold
\begin{equation}
  \tau^* = \frac{C_{\text{refault}}}{c_{\text{hold}} \cdot M_{\text{free}}}
\end{equation}
achieves a worst-case competitive ratio of at most $2$ against an offline clairvoyant oracle across any sequence of idle gaps $\{g_j\}$, with zero false reclaims whenever active inter-frame latency satisfies $t_{\text{frame}} < \tau^*$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each idle gap $g_j$ is an independent instance of the classic ski-rental problem~\cite{karlin1994competitive}: holding costs $c_{\text{hold}} M_{\text{free}} \cdot t$ continuously, while purging incurs a one-time buy cost $C_{\text{refault}}$ that zeroes further holding cost. An offline adversary pays $\text{OPT}(g_j) = \min(c_{\text{hold}} M_{\text{free}} g_j,\; C_{\text{refault}})$. Our online controller waits $\tau^*$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item If $g_j < \tau^*$ (including all active frames when $t_{\text{frame}} < \tau^*$), the watchdog never fires, so $\text{ALG}(g_j) = c_{\text{hold}} M_{\text{free}} g_j = \text{OPT}(g_j)$ (competitive ratio $1.0$ and $0$ false reclaims).
  \item If $g_j \ge \tau^*$, the controller pays holding cost $c_{\text{hold}} M_{\text{free}} \tau^* = C_{\text{refault}}$ plus purge/refault cost $C_{\text{refault}}$, totaling $2 C_{\text{refault}} = 2 \cdot \text{OPT}(g_j)$.
\end{itemize}
Hence $\text{ALG}(g_j) / \text{OPT}(g_j) \le 2$ for every $g_j \ge 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Multi-Stream Staggered Duty-Cycle Scaling]
\label{thm:multicam}
Let $N$ inference streams share a host with static per-stream framework baseline $R_{\text{base}}(N)$ and per-stream reclaimable arena body $M_{\text{arena}}$, where at most $K \le N$ streams execute concurrently (duty cycle $d = K/N$). Static retention (\texttt{pool\_retain}) incurs steady-state residency $R_{\text{static}} \approx R_{\text{base}}(N) + N \cdot M_{\text{arena}}$. Under synchronous cooperative arbitration (\texttt{GlobalBudgetArbiter} with global free-body budget $B_G = 0$), shedding oldest-idle streams at wave handoff limits resident arenas to the $K$ active streams:
\begin{equation}
  R_{\text{coop}}(N, K) \approx R_{\text{base}}(N) + K \cdot M_{\text{arena}},
\end{equation}
yielding arena-body reduction $(N-K)/N$ and avoiding inter-wave idle timer refaults when streams hand off sequentially.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At any wave transition where $K$ streams enter \texttt{mark\_frame\_start()}, at least $N - K$ streams satisfy \texttt{is\_idle() == true}. Because \texttt{notify\_burst\_start()} invokes \texttt{enforce\_budget()} with $B_G = 0$ sorted by \texttt{is\_idle} and oldest activity timestamp, all $N-K$ idle streams execute \texttt{pressure\_reclaim(0)}, reducing their free body to $0$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation} while leaving the $K$ active streams untouched ($0$ mid-burst refaults). Thus exactly $K$ streams retain $M_{\text{arena}}$ bytes of body pages, giving $R_{\text{coop}}(N, K) \approx R_{\text{base}}(N) + K \cdot M_{\text{arena}}$. In contrast, an asynchronous timer with threshold $\tau > 0$ and inter-wave gap $g > \tau$ reclaims even the last wave during the gap, incurring $N$ vs.\ $N-K$ wave-boundary refault cycles per round-robin cycle.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Linear Fault--Budget Tradeoff Law]
\label{thm:linear}
For a workload of $W$ burst cycles, each allocating peak free body $M_{\text{arena}}$ and returning to retained budget $B \in [0, M_{\text{arena}}]$ between bursts, total minor page faults $F(B)$ follow the linear law
\begin{equation}
  F(B) = F_0 + \frac{W \cdot \max(0, M_{\text{arena}} - B)}{P},
\end{equation}
yielding exact fault-reduction slope $\mathrm{d}F/\mathrm{d}B = -W/P$ pages per byte (i.e., $-256 W$ faults/MiB on \texttt{4~KiB} Linux and $-64 W$ faults/MiB on \texttt{16~KiB} macOS).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
During each idle gap, \texttt{purge\_to\_budget(B)} unbacks $(M_{\text{arena}} - B)^+ / P$ interior pages (Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation}). On the subsequent burst, deterministic tensor execution re-touches the working set, triggering one kernel minor page fault per unbacked page before reaching peak $M_{\text{arena}}$. Summing over $W$ identical bursts plus a fixed initialization fault floor $F_0$ yields $F(B) = F_0 + W (M_{\text{arena}} - B)^+ / P$, which is strictly linear with slope $-W/P$ for $B \le M_{\text{arena}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hybrid Cooperative + Online Idle Dominance]
\label{thm:hybrid}
Combining synchronous \texttt{GlobalBudgetArbiter} ($B_G=0$) with per-stream online idle timers (\texttt{pool\_hybrid}, threshold $\tau > 0$) strictly dominates each mechanism alone across the full inter-wave gap spectrum $g \ge 0$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Under back-to-back handoffs ($g < \tau$), synchronous arbitration prevents timer-lag overlap, capping peak footprint at $R_{\text{base}}(N) + K \cdot M_{\text{arena}}$ instead of the $(K + \min(K, N-K)) \cdot M_{\text{arena}}$ overlap spike of \texttt{pool\_auto\_0}.
  \item Under sparse bursts ($g \ge \tau$), the idle watchdog reclaims the final active wave during quiescent gaps, reducing idle footprint to $R_{\text{base}}(N)$ instead of $R_{\text{base}}(N) + K \cdot M_{\text{arena}}$ (\texttt{pool\_coop}).
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
When $g < \tau$, the timer on the previous wave's $K$ streams has not expired when the next $K$ streams call \texttt{mark\_frame\_start()}. Under \texttt{pool\_auto\_0}, both sets of $K$ streams are simultaneously resident until $\tau$ elapses, creating a $2K \cdot M_{\text{arena}}$ peak (when $2K \le N$). Under \texttt{pool\_hybrid}, \texttt{notify\_burst\_start()} invokes \texttt{pressure\_reclaim(0)} on the previous $K$ streams \emph{before} the incoming wave faults its working set, preserving the $K \cdot M_{\text{arena}}$ ceiling of Theorem~\ref{thm:multicam}. Conversely, when $g \ge \tau$, all $N$ streams are idle for $\ge \tau$, so the watchdog triggers \texttt{purge\_to\_budget(0)} on the remaining $K$ streams, matching \texttt{pool\_auto\_0}'s quiescent floor $R_{\text{base}}(N)$.
\end{proof}

\section{System Design}
\label{sec:design}

Figure~\ref{fig:fig5} illustrates the three-layer architecture implemented in \texttt{first\_steps/bench/utilization/}:

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../figures/fig5_architecture_arbiter.pdf}
  \caption{Cooperative multi-stream architecture connecting ONNX Runtime \texttt{OrtAllocator} sessions to per-stream \texttt{IdleReclamationController}s, the \texttt{GlobalBudgetArbiter}, and OS-specific page reclamation backends.}
  \label{fig:fig5}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{1. Cross-OS \texttt{PageReuseResource}.} Maintains address-ordered free blocks with immediate boundary coalescing and exact page-class accounting (\texttt{resident}, \texttt{resident\_body}, \texttt{resident\_head}, \texttt{resident\_tail}, \texttt{resident\_sub}, \texttt{live\_requested}). On Linux (\texttt{4~KiB}), \texttt{purge\_to\_budget(B)} calls \texttt{madvise} with \texttt{MADV\_DONTNEED} on full interior pages; on macOS (\texttt{16~KiB}), it calls \texttt{mmap} with \texttt{MAP\_FIXED} and \texttt{MAP\_ANON}.

\paragraph{2. \texttt{IdleReclamationController}.} Wraps \texttt{PageReuseResource} behind a lightweight mutex and a background watchdog thread. Worker threads bracket each inference call with \texttt{mark\_frame\_start} and \texttt{mark\_frame\_end}, updating an atomic in-flight counter and activity epoch. The watchdog only triggers \texttt{purge\_to\_budget} when no frames are in flight and elapsed idle time exceeds $\tau$, guaranteeing zero false reclaims during active bursts.

\paragraph{3. \texttt{GlobalBudgetArbiter}.} Coordinates $N$ registered controllers without lock-order inversion: \texttt{enforce\_budget} snapshots controller pointers under \texttt{arbiter\_mu\_}, releases the lock, queries per-stream free body bytes, and if the global sum exceeds $B_G$, sorts victims by idle state, oldest activity timestamp, and largest free body, invoking \texttt{pressure\_reclaim} strictly on idle streams.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We evaluate live ONNX Runtime C++ YOLO26n~\cite{ultralytics2026yolo26} inference (\texttt{1x3x640x640} FP32) with full tensor verification against platform-native golden references (\texttt{max\_abs\_error} $= 0$ across every reported run). Because x86\_64 AVX2 and arm64 NEON produce hardware-level floating-point ordering differences on NMS-free YOLO26n boxes, each platform verifies against its own native reference binary, and Linux \texttt{4~KiB} and macOS \texttt{16~KiB} numbers are kept strictly separated.

\subsection{Platform A: CloudLab \texttt{m510} Bare-Metal Linux (\texttt{4~KiB})}
On CloudLab \texttt{m510} (Intel Xeon D-1548, 8 cores, Linux \texttt{6.8.0}, \texttt{4~KiB} pages, pinned core, ONNX Runtime \texttt{1.24.4}), we first note an important methodological boundary from our early single-stream screening: while \texttt{PageReuseResource} achieves $99.9903\%$ allocator-local utilization on microbenchmarks, in continuous active inference \texttt{direct\_trim} slightly edges out \texttt{pool\_eager\_trim} on single-stream active peak RSS ($73.39$ vs.\ $73.74\text{ MiB}$) due to framework-level buffers. The strength of our bounded pool emerges under idle-aware burst and endurance workloads.

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:fig2}, pairing \texttt{pool\_idle} with a \texttt{24~MiB} retained budget across 5 paired trials (\texttt{confirm\_*} in \texttt{results/bounded/}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item In bursty inference (\texttt{1000~ms} pause), idle private RSS drops to $0.4916\times$ (paired-ratio median) and private byte-seconds drop to $0.5604\times$, while burst $p_{99}$ latency stays at $1.0108\times$ (\texttt{pool\_retain} parity).
  \item In continuous inference (\texttt{0~ms} pause), $p_{99}$ latency remains within $1.0182\times$ (paired-ratio median).
  \item Over a 1,000-frame endurance run (100 scheduled \texttt{1~s} pauses), idle private RSS drops by $50.9\%$ ($58.37$ vs.\ $118.79\text{ MiB}$) at virtually identical throughput ($5.751$ vs.\ $5.796\text{ FPS}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Platform B: Apple M2 Pro (\texttt{16~KiB}) Single-Stream Auto-Idle}
On an Apple M2 Pro MacBook Pro (12 cores, \texttt{32~GiB} RAM, Darwin \texttt{25.6.0}, \texttt{clang++ 23.1.2}, ONNX Runtime \texttt{1.32.0}, \texttt{16~KiB} pages), we executed 46 protocol-validated runs (\texttt{auto\_idle\_bench}, 5 trials per confirmed configuration, \texttt{max\_abs\_error} $= 0$, Figure~\ref{fig:fig1}(a)):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Continuous regime:} \texttt{pool\_auto} triggers \textbf{0 false reclaims} and \textbf{0 total reclaims}, matching Theorem~\ref{thm:competitive} ($74.0\text{ MiB}$ vs.\ $105.8\text{ MiB}$ \texttt{BFC}).
  \item \textbf{Burst (\texttt{1000~ms} gap) \& Mixed (\texttt{500~ms} gap):} \texttt{pool\_auto (0~MiB)} drops idle \texttt{phys\_footprint} by $60.0\%$ ($42.25\text{--}42.31\text{ MiB}$ vs.\ $105.75\text{--}106.02\text{ MiB}$ \texttt{BFC}) with \textbf{0 false reclaims}, $12/12$ idle windows reclaimed in burst, and burst $p_{99}$ latency ratio $1.013\times$ vs.\ \texttt{BFC}. In mixed workloads, adaptive idle detection halves minor faults compared to naive explicit reclamation ($12,114$ vs.\ $24,229$).
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:multicam} and Figure~\ref{fig:fig4} reveal three key findings:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Major Footprint Reduction:} Both \texttt{pool\_auto\_0} and \texttt{pool\_coop} cut mean \texttt{phys\_footprint} by $62\%\text{--}66\%$ relative to \texttt{BFC} ($122.8\text{--}126.1$ vs.\ $332.2\text{ MiB}$ for $N=4$; $216.5\text{--}224.8$ vs.\ $635.8\text{ MiB}$ for $N=8$) and by $43\%\text{--}48\%$ relative to \texttt{pool\_retain}.
  \item \textbf{Cooperative vs.\ Timer Tradeoff:} Because \texttt{burst\_gap\_ms} ($350\text{ ms}$) exceeds \texttt{idle\_ms} ($200\text{ ms}$), \texttt{pool\_auto\_0} reclaims during inter-wave gaps too, yielding $3\text{--}8\text{ MiB}$ lower mean footprint, whereas \texttt{pool\_coop} keeps the latest wave warm until the next wave arrives---cutting minor page faults by $12.4\%$ ($14{,}193$ vs.\ $16{,}207$ and $28{,}387$ vs.\ $32{,}417$) with zero watchdog threads.
  \item \textbf{Apple Silicon P-Core Wakeup Effect:} While \texttt{wall\_sec} ($\approx 8.60\text{ s}$) and median latency $p_{50}$ ($80.0\text{--}84.5\text{ ms}$) are virtually identical across all policies, \texttt{pool\_coop} exhibits lower per-frame $p_{99}$ ($109.6$ and $102.6\text{ ms}$ vs.\ $127.8$ and $128.7\text{ ms}$ for \texttt{pool\_retain}) because synchronous arbiter reclamation immediately before frame $0$ wakes the P-core from idle DVFS/C-state down-clocking prior to timing. We report this transparently as a hardware frequency-ramp effect rather than an algorithmic speedup.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Threats to Validity}
General-purpose allocators such as \texttt{jemalloc}~\cite{evans2006jemalloc}, \texttt{TCMalloc}~\cite{ghemawat2005tcmalloc}, and \texttt{mimalloc}~\cite{leijen2019mimalloc} optimize multi-threaded throughput and use time-based decay purging, but lack neural-burst frame instrumentation and cross-stream cooperative budgets. Fistanto et al.~\cite{fistanto2023lostbytes} diagnosed OS granularity mismatch, while Berger et al.~\cite{berger2002reconsidering} and van Kempen and Berger~\cite{ismm2024reconsidering} examined region allocation and heap-state evaluation pitfalls. Our work builds directly on these foundations for dedicated neural inference.

\paragraph{Threats to Validity.} First, \texttt{auto\_idle\_bench}, \texttt{multicam\_\allowbreak staggered\_\allowbreak bench}, and \texttt{stochastic\_ski\_bench} have been executed on Apple M2 Pro (\texttt{16~KiB}); bare-metal CloudLab \texttt{4~KiB} replication of these newer harnesses remains pending future work. Second, our \texttt{16~KiB} \texttt{mmap(MAP\_FIXED)} path relies on POSIX anonymous remapping preserving virtual address reservations; while verified safe under ASan/UBSan and 181 live ORT runs, its cost scales with the number of discontiguous interior spans.

\section{Conclusion}
By unifying exact boundary-aware page-grid accounting, OS-appropriate physical unbacking (\texttt{MADV\_DONTNEED} on Linux \texttt{4~KiB}; \texttt{mmap(MAP\_FIXED)} on macOS \texttt{16~KiB}), 2-competitive idle-threshold detection, and synchronous cooperative multi-stream arbitration, we cut physical memory footprint by $50.9\%$ on CloudLab endurance runs and by $60\%\text{--}66\%$ across single- and multi-camera Apple M2 Pro deployments with bit-exact outputs and tight theoretical predictability ($0.36\%$ multi-camera error; $R^2 \ge 0.99987$ fault scaling).
